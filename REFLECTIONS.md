# Reflections

1. **Stakeholder disagreement.** I expect the operations manager to disagree most sharply with the sustainability and strategy managers: opening a warehouse may improve distance and emissions, but adds operating and investment costs.

2. **Assumption I would least defend.** The transport and storage cost figures are illustrative and unsourced commercial estimates. Unlike the CO₂ factor, they do not have a cited range behind them.

3. **Different KPIs, different actions.** Lyon has the highest annual cost at €13,476,013, so total cost says to investigate it first. Its cost breakdown is €7,788,952 for storage versus €5,687,061 for transport, pointing to storage and inventory before route-cost changes.

4. **Trust in the top CO₂ route.** The top route is Lyon to Marseille: 277.6 km and 1,211,829.5 kg CO₂/year, about 19.4% of the network total. I would use it to prioritize an investigation, not treat it as definitive, because demand is estimated from population and distances are straight-line rather than actual shipment routes.

5. **First cost lever.** I would address storage at Lyon first: its €7,788,952 annual storage estimate exceeds its €5,687,061 transport estimate. Both figures depend on illustrative cost assumptions.

6. **Network recommendation.** I would test opening Marseille: its scenario reduces population-weighted average distance to 115.03 km from the baseline 168.04 km. Confidence is limited because the comparison omits opening/lease costs and Lille’s cross-border access; those omissions could change the decision. My prediction that operations would push back partly holds, since the distance result favors opening Marseille but does not show whether it pays off.

7. **Fourth chart/KPI.** I added a KPI to the CO₂ dashboard showing the top route’s share of total network emissions. It answers how concentrated emissions are on one route, which the total and average do not. A percentage KPI fits that question directly; Lyon to Marseille currently contributes 19.4% of the network total.
